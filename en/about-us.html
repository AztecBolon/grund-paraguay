About Us: a clear agreement, fixed prices, a real team

We solve one narrow problem: how to move to Paraguay in a way that means, two years from now, you're not left wondering why your residency wasn't renewed, why the tax office charged a penalty for a zero return you never filed, or why the land you bought turned out to be non-transferable. That's not a matter of luck or connections — it's a matter of discipline in handling documents, and a clear agreement that sets out scope and price for both sides.

How exactly we assess risks and talk about them plainly is on the "Risks & Difficulties" page.

Team and authority

We operate as Fortuna Vida E.A.S. and work with local partners on the ground — lawyers and notaries (escribanos) who handle transactions under Paraguayan law, not remotely from Russia. This matters in particular for real-estate due diligence and for company registration, where the legal representative is required by law to hold Paraguayan citizenship or a Cédula. We publish our team roster as materials become ready — openness on this point matters to us as much as a fixed price and an honest timeline (on why, conversely, we don't publish client names, see the "Confidentiality" page).

Agreement and terms

We work under a written agreement that fixes the scope of services and the price before work begins — no floating "depends on complexity" pricing, which on this market often means add-on charges once the client is already committed to the process. We do not promise a specific result or timeline: decisions are made by the authorities, and the timelines on this site are based on our experience and may vary in an individual case — that is a business risk no agency can honestly take on. The full price of each service is on the "Costs and Rates" page. We discuss and show the terms of the agreement at the consultation, before it's signed.

What this looks like in practice — in the "Case Studies" section: illustrative scenarios (not real client cases), including an honest conversation about the difficulties of the first year. The step-by-step process and an open conversation about risks are on the "How We Work" page.

We don't promise citizenship "in a year," and we don't blend the government fee with our own fee into a single advertised number — both points appear as separate lines in the written agreement, not just in text on the site. Ready to discuss your situation? Leave a request.
