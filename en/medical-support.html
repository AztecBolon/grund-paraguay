Medical support in Paraguay

Medical support isn't the policy itself — it's help with everything around it: choosing a plan that fits your status, not missing a critical condition in the contract, and not being left alone at a clinic's front desk in Spanish. There's no separate government service for "insurance for foreigners" in Paraguay — we put the result together from the plans already on the market and our own work around them.

We cover Paraguay's healthcare system in detail on a separate page, "Healthcare in Paraguay" — here, in short: three levels (basic MSPBS care, the employment-linked IPS social insurance, private medicine), and for most people relocating the real question isn't "free or paid" but which private plan to choose and on what terms.

What the support includes

  • Plan selection — we compare offers from local insurers (e.g., MAPFRE Paraguay, Asismed) and international policies against your status and budget. Some local plans have conditions that rule out part of applicants right away — for example, MAPFRE Paraguay requires permanent-resident status; at the temporary-residency stage that plan simply isn't available, and it's better to know that in advance, not after a rejection.
  • Reading the fine print — waiting periods ("carencias") before specific conditions are covered, the mandatory health declaration at signup, the list of exclusions. Local plans' terms can be quite strict: for example, one widespread plan requires declaring chronic conditions in advance (6 months' waiting period for declared ones, at least 12 for cancer and kidney/liver disease, and undeclared conditions aren't covered at all).
  • Support at appointments — translation, help with paperwork on the spot, and explaining the bill: private clinics in Paraguay usually require payment right away, without waiting for reimbursement from the insurer.
  • Help filing a claim — deadlines for submitting claim documents can be short (48 hours for some plans), and we make sure you don't miss that deadline in a foreign country in an unfamiliar language.

Why this actually matters

Dengue is a real seasonal risk, not a rarity (more detail and sources on the "Myths & Questions" page, "Health & Climate" section). The UK's official travel portal notes directly that quality private medical care in Paraguay is mostly available in major cities, and private clinics usually require immediate on-the-spot payment — meaning insurance isn't "just in case," it's a working tool you'll likely need in your first year.

What's not included at the start

Setting up IPS (for those formally employed in Paraguay or registering a microenterprise) is a separate process through the employer, outside the scope of this service at the outset; we discuss it separately if needed, rather than bundling it in by default.

Cost

We didn't find an offer on the market where insurance arrangement and support are sold as one service specifically for people relocating to Paraguay — there simply isn't an established price benchmark for that. Pricing for the insurance plans themselves, from both local and international insurers, isn't published publicly (it's calculated individually by age and health status) — we calculate the scope and cost of our own support individually too, once your status is clear and which plan is under consideration.
