Banking, Accounts, and Money Transfers

How to open an account in Paraguay, what's known about sanctions restrictions on transfers from Russia, and why crypto isn't a risk-free workaround.

Sanctions-related banking restrictions keep changing throughout 2026 — when planning, it's worth checking the current status separately.

It's worth starting with the position of the Central Bank of Paraguay (BCP) itself: cryptocurrencies there officially have no status as legal tender, aren't registered or authorized by the regulator, and are explicitly labeled high-risk assets. Since fiscal year 2026, mandatory reporting on crypto assets has applied in the country: platforms must report through the tax system Marangatú, and residents and legal entities must declare transactions if their annual turnover exceeds $5,000; the first such returns will need to be filed for 2026, in early 2027, with a late-filing penalty of about $130. The takeaway is direct: crypto and stablecoins aren't a risk-free workaround for moving money — they're an area falling under increasingly tight regulation.

Separately, it's worth settling the reputational question: as of mid-2026, Paraguay isn't on the FATF (the international anti-money-laundering group) "grey list" — this directly refutes any concerns about elevated banking risk specifically because of the country's status.

As for opening an account — going by one bank's example (Itaú Paraguay, via data from the fintech service Wise, not the bank's own primary source), at the time of checking, a foreign-currency account required a minimum average balance of around $6,000 for a checking account and $2,500 for a savings account. Treat this specifically as an example of one bank's terms, not a general rule for all — different banks in Paraguay have different requirements, and the exact list needs to be confirmed right before opening an account.

A separate practical context is sanctions-related restrictions on transfers from Russia. SWIFT officially disconnected Sberbank back on June 3, 2022. Restrictions have since expanded: the EU's 19th sanctions package (10/23/2025) added five more Russian banks to the transaction ban, including Alfa-Bank and MTS Bank, and from January 25, 2026, restrictions also extended to the SPFS/SBP system and the "Mir" payment system; the EU's 20th sanctions package (04/23/2026) introduced a full ban on the ruble stablecoin and on support for developing the Russian Central Bank's digital ruble, adding another twenty Russian banks to the restrictions. Transferring money from Russia to Paraguay is a topic that calls for an individual review of the specific situation during a consultation, not a general instruction that applies to everyone.
