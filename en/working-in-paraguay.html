Working in Paraguay: how formal employment works

Whether you need residency to work legally, what the cédula gets you, and when to register for RUC and IPS — step by step, with sources and an honest note on what couldn't be confirmed.

Paraguay doesn't have a separate "work permit" independent of immigration status — the legal right to work comes from residency itself. The question isn't "which permit to get," it's which documents and registrations are needed at each step — from residency through to the labor contract. Paraguay also has no equivalent of a Russian-style "work record book": employment is evidenced by the labor contract and the social-insurance registration.

Residency — the mandatory precondition

The right to work legally comes from temporary (Residencia Temporal) or permanent (Residencia Permanente) residency — sources don't describe a separate "work visa" track apart from residency. Without valid residency, formal employment isn't possible. The document checklist for applying is on the "Documents for Temporary Residency" page.

Paraguayan cédula de identidad

Since August 2024, the Paraguayan cédula has been available already at temporary-resident status — previously it was available only to permanent residents. A recent (as of this check) list of requirements for a foreigner:

  1. Certificado de Radicación (Permanent or Temporary) from the immigration service (DNM)
  2. a certified copy of the Carnet de Admisión
  3. a certified copy of the home-country ID document with a foreign-ministry visa stamp
  4. birth certificate — original, with consular legalization and an apostille
  5. civil-status documents, where applicable
  6. Certificado de Vida y Residencia from the local police commissariat or justice of the peace
  7. a police-clearance certificate from the home country (for anyone age 14 or older)
  8. an Interpol clearance certificate
  9. a police-clearance certificate from Paraguay itself
  10. a certified copy of the registration card (registro)

Documents not in Spanish need translation by a registered sworn translator (Traductor Público matriculado).

RUC — tax registration

Once Residencia Temporal is granted, registering an RUC and filing tax returns becomes mandatory — this applies to salaried employees and entrepreneurs alike, only the set of documents proving your occupation differs. Rates and the underlying tax logic are covered on the "Taxes for Foreigners" page.

IPS and the labor contract with an employer

Formalizing the employment relationship with a contract is mandatory under the Labor Code (Ley N° 213/93) and Ley N° 3452/2008 — for citizens and foreigners alike. Separately, registration with IPS is mandatory (Art. 95 of Paraguay's Constitution): the contribution is 25.5% of salary, split 9% employee / 16.5% employer. On the employee's side, registration needs the carnet de migraciones plus an ID document (the home-country document, until the cédula is issued). On the employer's side, registering with IPS needs their cédula and RUC/Constancia de RUC (for an individual employer) or founding documents, minutes of the most recent shareholders' meeting, and the representative's cédula (for a legal entity).

We couldn't find a single official checklist of documents for the labor contract itself (unlike the clear lists for the cédula and IPS registration above) — its content (terms, pay, duties) appears to be governed substantively by the Labor Code rather than by a separate paperwork list. We also couldn't confirm a specific intergovernmental labor-migration agreement between Russia and Paraguay — employment is handled under the general regime described above, the same one that applies to all foreigners.
