Citizenship: Documents for Naturalization

Naturalization is possible only 3 years after obtaining permanent residency (Art. 148 of the Constitution). The case is handled not by the migration service but by the Supreme Court (Corte Suprema de Justicia) — the document list below was taken directly from the official Poder Judicial page.

Primary sourceThe document list comes from the official Poder Judicial page "Carta de Naturalización", not a summary. Confidence: high.

Documents for obtaining the Carta de Naturalización

  1. Passport-style photos — front and profile, 15×10 cm format.
  2. A certified copy of your Paraguayan ID card (Cédula).
  3. A certified copy of your passport — all pages, including blank ones.
  4. Birth certificate — certified copy, apostilled.
  5. A certified copy of the permanent resident card issued by DNM.
  6. A residency-status certificate from DNM — 3 years of permanent-resident status from the date it was obtained.
  7. A criminal record certificate from Paraguay's police identification authorities.
  8. A criminal record certificate from Paraguay's judicial branch.
  9. A good-conduct certificate from your home country — apostilled.
  10. Documents proving your occupation — these vary by category: employees — a letter from the employer with IPS and RUC numbers; entrepreneurs — a commercial license and RUC details; students — an enrollment certificate; professionals — qualification documents.
  11. A résumé (curriculum vitae).
  12. A sworn statement, signed in person at the Supreme Court.
  13. A court fee — ₲43,051.
  14. A written naturalization petition, filed with a lawyer's support, stating your permanent address in Asunción.

Additional conditions: the applicant must be of legal age; appearing in person to sign the sworn statement is mandatory; all documents must be originals or certified copies; documents not in Spanish must be translated by a translator registered with the Supreme Court.

Open questionThe official page doesn't mention a Spanish/Guaraní language exam or a Paraguayan history exam — though in 2019 the Senate approved a bill along these lines, and its current status (passed or not) hasn't been established. We look into this individually if it turns out to be relevant by the time you apply.

Source: the official Poder Judicial page "Carta de Naturalización" (primary source, government domain). Confidence on the document list — high; on the language exam question — unresolved.
