The Itaipú Dam

One of the world's largest hydroelectric plants, on the border with Brazil — it supplies about 80% of Paraguay's electricity, and visiting from the Paraguayan side is free.

PhotoWe don't have our own photos yet — see a selection for "Itaipú dam Paraguay Brazil" on Google Images →

Itaipú is a hydroelectric plant on the Paraná River, a joint project of Paraguay and Brazil. The dam is 7,235 m long and 196 m high (comparable to a 65-story building), with an installed capacity of 14 GW (20 turbines of 700 MW each). As of 2020 it was the world's second-largest hydroelectric plant by energy output, after China's Three Gorges Dam: in the record year of 2016, output came to 103.1 TWh, averaging 89.22 TWh a year over 2012–2021. The plant supplies about 80% of Paraguay's electricity consumption and roughly 20% of Brazil's. Construction began in 1975, the first generator went online in 1984, and the last ones in 2006–2007. The name comes from Guaraní and translates as "the singing stone"; in 1994 the American Society of Civil Engineers named Itaipú one of the seven wonders of the modern world.

Visiting from the Paraguayan side is free: a short film and a bus tour (the guide speaks Spanish, the film has English subtitles); a separate "technical" tour is available by prior booking. The easiest way there is from Ciudad del Este by scheduled bus to Hernandarias — the timetable and exact visiting conditions are worth checking right before the trip, as such details change.
