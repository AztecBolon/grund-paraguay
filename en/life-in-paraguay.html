Life in Paraguay

Asunción's climate month by month, neighborhoods of the capital and its metro area, the German colonies in the south of the country, and Mennonites from Russia in the Chaco — a practical picture of everyday life, not a tourist brochure.

Asunción's climate is subtropical, with a marked contrast between summer and winter. According to averaged data from weather aggregators, the summer months (January) hold a daytime high of around 32–33°C, while winter (July, the coldest month) runs 14–22°C during the day with cooler nights; annual rainfall averages around 1,629 mm. Different aggregators give slightly different figures, which is why it makes sense to give a range here rather than a single number.

Asunción itself is divided into 68 official neighborhoods (barrios); the city's population per the 2022 census is 462,241, and Greater Asunción's is over 2.4 million — about 40% of the country's entire population. We couldn't find an independent (non-realtor) review of specific neighborhoods by quality of life — all similar content in the open web turned out to be marketing from real estate agencies and expat services, so there's no "top 5 neighborhoods to live in" ranking here.

The south of the country is a separate, non-touristy reason why Paraguay might sound more familiar than it first appears. German colonization of the Itapúa region began with the town of Hohenau, founded in 1900 (the land-grant decision was made back in 1898); later, Obligado (1912); both are part of the "Colonias Unidas" cluster. In the 1930s–40s, Polish, Ukrainian, and Russian settlers joined the German colonists, followed by Japanese settlers from 1958. This is one established community with nearly a century of mixed European immigration history, not an abstract "Germany in South America."

A separate and larger community is the roughly 25,000 Mennonites in Boquerón department, in the Chaco region. They're descendants of Dutch-Prussian settlers who arrived in Paraguay specifically from Russia — first fleeing the abolition of military service exemptions in the 19th century, then Stalinist collectivization in the 20th. It's important not to mix up these two German-speaking communities when talking with a client: the southern colonies (Itapúa) are a century-old mixed European colonization, while the Mennonites of the Chaco are a separate community with their own, specifically Russian, backstory.

This page is a starting map, not a housing rental guide: for a specific neighborhood, the climate of a specific month of relocation, or the way of life in a region you're interested in, it makes more sense to book a free consultation than to rely on averaged aggregator figures.
