Taxes for Foreigners in Paraguay

Paraguay's territorial tax principle and IRE, IVA, IRP, and IDU rates explained in plain terms — and an honest note that no double taxation treaty with Russia could be found.

Paraguay's tax system is based on Law No. 6380/2019; the tax authority itself has recently been officially renamed DNIT (successor to the former SET) — this is the agency where returns are physically filed and that an accountant deals with after obtaining an RUC.

Main rates as of mid-2026: corporate income tax, IRE, — 10%; VAT, IVA, — 10% standard rate and 5% reduced rate; dividend tax (IDU) — 8% for Paraguayan tax residents and 15% for non-residents; personal income tax, IRP, on personal services — tiered at 8/9/10% depending on the amount, with an annual return due in March.

One principle is worth understanding up front, because it directly affects anyone who keeps earning income from abroad after relocating: taxation in Paraguay generally follows the territorial principle — foreign-source income of individual residents generally isn't included in the IRP tax base. We didn't find direct confirmation of this rule in the primary text of the law — treat it as likely practice rather than a guarantee, and check it separately for your specific situation.

Separately — the question of a double taxation treaty: between Paraguay and Russia, at the time of checking, none could be found in open sources. That doesn't guarantee one doesn't exist — but in its absence, income of Russian origin for a Paraguayan resident may by default be taxed in both jurisdictions without mutual offset. For Paraguay, treaties are confirmed with at least Chile, Uruguay, Germany, Belgium, Taiwan, the UAE, and Qatar — for any specific case, a separate consultation makes more sense than a general conclusion drawn from this page.
