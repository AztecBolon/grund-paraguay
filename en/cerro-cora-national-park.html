Cerro Corá National Park

Paraguay's largest protected area, on the border with Brazil — site of the final battle of the Paraguayan War and ancient petroglyphs.

PhotoWe don't have our own photos yet — see a selection for "Cerro Corá National Park Paraguay" on Google Images →

Cerro Corá is Paraguay's largest national park, about 56.5 km², in the department of Amambay, near the border with Brazil. In 1870, the final battle of the Paraguayan War was fought here, in which Marshal Francisco Solano López was killed — memorials to the event stand within the park, and nearby ancient petroglyphs and rock art of indigenous peoples have survived.

The landscape features the Amambay hills, the Aquidabán River, caves, subtropical forest, and savanna; the fauna recorded includes jaguars, pumas, ocelots, tapirs, capybaras, howler monkeys, harpy eagles, and toucans (the exact species list varies in detail between sources). Hiking, caving, birdwatching, camping, and riverside picnics are all possible — but this is the far northeast of the country, closer to Pedro Juan Caballero than to Asunción, and the trip needs separate planning.
