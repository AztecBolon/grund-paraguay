Honestly: risks and difficulties of moving to Paraguay

This page isn't meant to scare you — it's meant to manage expectations: something that should be part of the service from the start, not something you find out later. If you've already read the "Process & Timeline" page, this is the same commitment to honesty, applied to risks instead of timelines.

Fraud in the relocation market

The market for relocation intermediary services to Latin America has a generally toxic reputation, and Paraguay is no exception. That said, we couldn't find a published official warning from the migration service (DNM) specifically addressing fraudulent intermediary agents, of the kind other migration services in the region have issued. That doesn't mean one doesn't exist: part of the DNM's official resources were unavailable when we gathered this information, and we couldn't confirm its text or date directly.

An isolated case, not a statisticSeparately, there's a documented case where the DNM found forged documents from an applicant and decided to deport them — but that's an example of fraud by the applicant themselves, not an agency defrauding a client; the details of this case also aren't confirmed by a primary source, so we mention it as an isolated precedent, not a statistic.

The practical takeaway: any offer to "speed up" or "get around" the process through non-standard means is worth checking independently, not taking on faith just because someone mentioned it in a chat — our contract and fixed terms are described on the "About Us" page.

Health and climate

Dengue is a real seasonal risk, not a rarity. Throughout 2025, Paraguay recorded 31,656 suspected dengue cases, of which 1,374 were lab-confirmed and 1 was fatal (451 cases per 100,000 population); in the first four weeks of 2026 alone, there were already 2,412 cases with an upward trend (source: PAHO, report dated 02/18/2026). Meanwhile, a more recent Paraguayan Ministry of Health report from January 9, 2026 described the picture as "stable, with low viral circulation" — just 3 confirmed cases over 3 weeks, with about 465 suspected cases reported per week nationwide. The two reports don't contradict each other: they cover different periods and different levels of diagnostic confirmation, and both say the same thing — the risk is seasonal and calls for standard prevention, not panic.

The UK's official travel portal (GOV.UK) directly names dengue and chikungunya as current risks for those entering Paraguay, and separately notes: good-quality private medical care is mainly available in major cities, private clinics usually require immediate payment on the spot, and medical insurance with emergency evacuation coverage is strongly recommended. Worth factoring into your first-year budget planning — a detailed cost breakdown is on the "Pricing" page.

Needs verificationAccording to some data (whose currency for 2026 wasn't re-confirmed in this pass — the figures date to 2022), entering Paraguay from Bolivia, Brazil, Peru, or Venezuela may require a yellow fever vaccination certificate for people aged 1–59 — worth confirming separately before travel if your route passes through one of those countries.

One practical point: access to state health insurance (IPS) in Paraguay is tied to formal employment and permanent-resident status; temporary residents don't get automatic access — meaning private insurance is almost always needed for the first years, regardless of residency status.

Safety

Per official statistics, 2025 closed with 391 homicides nationwide (6.1 per 100,000 population) — down 18.6% from 2024 (477 cases, 7.5 per 100,000); the third-best safety figure in Latin America after Argentina and Chile (sources: InSight Crime, La Nación, June 2026). The US State Department's Overseas Security Advisory Council (OSAC), in a report dated 05/27/2025, rated the threat level for Asunción as high for street crime, medium for political violence, and low for terrorism; contract killings dropped from 110 cases in 2019 to 18 in 2024, and kidnappings of foreigners are quite rare (per this source, no cases involving US citizens since 2011), though "virtual kidnapping" phone scams are on the rise.

The US State Department's overall travel advisory level for Paraguay is the baseline level ("exercise normal precautions"), but five northeastern departments on the Brazilian border are flagged for heightened caution due to smuggling and limited police presence:

This is an orientation diagram, not cartographically precise — per the US State Department; the page with this assessment is dated 04/30/2025, meaning it's over a year old as of this writing and worth re-checking for currency before publishing.

Needs verificationIn the Ciudad del Este / Alto Paraná border region, sources note the presence of Brazilian criminal groups and local clans, and Amambay department's homicide rate is estimated to be notably above the national average — the exact current figures for these two points are outdated in the sources (data partly from 2019 and 2022) and need separate verification before use in specific consultations.

Per the Global Organized Crime Index, Paraguay is a major cocaine transit hub and South America's largest cannabis producer, with a criminality score of 7.48 out of 10 (4th highest worldwide) and low system resilience against crime. Per Transparency International's 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index, Paraguay scores 24 out of 100 (150th worldwide) — below the Americas average of 42/100, unchanged from 2024.

None of these figures mean moving to Paraguay is riskier than the regional average — most statistics point the other way. But the border departments and general caution with personal data while traveling are real, not hypothetical, factors worth knowing in advance.

Regulatory window

On July 6, 2026, DNM resolution N° 407/2026 took effect — stricter financial-capacity checks across 12 applicant categories for Residencia Permanente. Since July 1, 2026, new DNM government fees have also applied (Decreto 6225/2026 and Resolución DNM N° 478/2026); these fees are indexed annually alongside the minimum wage — meaning this isn't a one-time hike but a regularly updated figure that needs re-checking every year around July 1 (current amounts are on the "Pricing" page). As an indicator of the broader policy direction: since January 10, 2026, entry has been tightened for Venezuelan citizens (Decreto N° 5278/2026) — this decision has no direct bearing on Russian citizens, but it shows that Paraguay's immigration policy in 2026 is generally moving toward tighter control, not liberalization. That's not a reason to panic, but it is a reasonable argument against putting off the decision indefinitely if a move is already being seriously considered — examples of how this played out for specific clients are in the "Case Studies" section.
