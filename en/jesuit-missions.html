The Jesuit missions of Trinidad and Jesús de Tavarangue

Paraguay's only UNESCO World Heritage Site — ruins of 17th–18th-century Jesuit missions near the border with Argentina.

PhotoWe don't have our own photos yet — see a selection for "Jesuit missions Trinidad Jesús de Tavarangue Paraguay" on Google Images →

La Santísima Trinidad de Paraná and Jesús de Tavarangue are two Jesuit mission (reducción) complexes inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993. Trinidad was founded in 1706, covers about 8 hectares, and is the best preserved of the two — the original layout is still visible: a church at the center, surrounded by dwellings, schools, workshops, and gardens. Jesús de Tavarangue was founded in 1685 and survives as archaeological ruins in the Mudéjar style. The protected area of the two sites together is 27.88 hectares, plus a buffer zone of 37.31 hectares.

The Jesuits arrived in the Guairá region in 1588 and, over the 17th and 18th centuries, founded 30 similar missions across the entire La Plata basin — the twin aim was to Christianize the indigenous population while also protecting it from colonial labor exploitation. After the Jesuits were expelled in the 18th century, the missions were abandoned. This is the country's only UNESCO site — good reason to include it on your route if you're heading southeast.
