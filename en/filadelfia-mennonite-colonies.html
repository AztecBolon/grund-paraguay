Filadelfia and the Mennonite colonies

The administrative center of the Chaco region, founded by Mennonites who fled the USSR in the 1930s — a museum of the region's history and colonial architecture amid dry forest.

PhotoWe don't have our own photos yet — see a selection for "Filadelfia Chaco Paraguay Mennonite colony" on Google Images →

Filadelfia is the administrative center of the Chaco region, 467 km from Asunción (about 7 hours by road, reachable only overland — by bus or by car), 15 km from the Trans-Chaco highway. The colony was founded by Mennonite settlers in the 1920s and 1930s — the town itself was founded in 1930 as Colonia Fernheim; these are German-speaking Mennonites who fled the USSR, and the community still preserves Plautdietsch (Low German) to this day (the same historical story already described on the "Life in Paraguay" page in connection with the Mennonites of Boquerón).

The population is about 18,000: Mennonites make up roughly 30% and run most of the local business, while indigenous peoples (Nivaclé, Guaraní, Enlhet, Ayoreo) make up about 60%. Among the sights are the Jakob Unger Museum (the region's history and nature, Mennonite heritage), three monuments marking the colony's 25th, 50th, and 75th anniversaries, the Park of Remembrance for the first settlers, and colonial-era German architecture on the main street. A practical note: most businesses close from noon to 2 p.m. and on weekends, and the Chaco climate is extreme — it's worth planning ahead for water and clothing.
