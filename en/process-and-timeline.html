Process & timelines from experience: from moving to Paraguayan citizenship

The sequence of stages toward permanent status in Paraguay, with no shortcuts and no promises of "we'll handle it in a week." The timelines below are based on our experience and official sources; in an individual case they may vary. Details on what each stage involves are on the "Services" page.

  1. Stage 1. Temporary residency (Residencia Temporal)

    Processed under Ley N° 6984/22 "De Migraciones" (Art. 46) and valid for up to 2 years. If needed, it can be renewed for the same period. Status is lost after an absence from the country of more than 1 year. In our experience, processing Residencia Temporal usually takes about 8 weeks, but it can vary; after you receive the Cédula (CI), the RUC is issued in 3–6 days on average. Already at this stage, once you get residency, you must register a RUC and file tax returns regularly (usually monthly) — this is a separate support service (see the "Services" page), not a formality you can put off.

    Documents for this stage

  2. Stage 2. Window to apply for permanent residency

    The standard application for Residencia Permanente is filed within 90 days before Residencia Temporal expires — meaning the transition isn't automatic and requires a specific action within a specific window. The exception is the investor route via SUACE (see "Services"): with investments from 70,000 USD, permanent residency is available directly, without going through Temporal.

  3. Stage 3. Permanent residency (Residencia Permanente)

    Currently (the rules have been changing fairly quickly lately), permanent residency is first issued for a fixed term of 2 years and is extended to 10 years only through a separate renewal procedure; these rules may change again. The status is lost after an absence from the country of more than 3 consecutive years. Since July 6, 2026, Permanente applications are reviewed under the new DNM resolution N° 407/2026 — it introduces 12 applicant categories (professionals, employees, freelancers and digital nomads, merchants, service providers, property owners, business shareholders, farmers, clergy, retirees, dependents, students) with separate financial-capacity requirements for each. The exact thresholds per category haven't been officially published yet — we confirm them individually for your situation at the time of filing; current rates are on the "Pricing" page.

    Documents for this stage

  4. Stage 4. Citizenship

    Under Paraguay's Constitution (Art. 148), naturalization is only possible after 3 years of "radicación" (residency with status) — and this period is counted from the date you received Residencia Permanente, not from the date you entered the country. The naturalization case is handled by the Supreme Court (Corte Suprema de Justicia), not the migration service; the procedure requires the applicant to appear in person with legal representation.

    Documents for this stage

Where the paperwork is done

Clients do their paperwork at our office in Encarnación. If you don't plan to stay in the Hohenau, Obligado or Bella Vista area, we find another suitable place in Paraguay through other partners — more on the "Map of Paraguay" page.

5 years the theoretical minimum (2 years of Temporal + 3 years of waiting after Permanente) — the formal sum of stage timelines, not counting the real time needed to gather documents and process the case.

In practice, accounting for the real time it takes to gather documents, process the case, and get it through the Supreme Court, DNM director Jorge Cronovetter publicly gave a more honest figure in May 2026 — 7 to 8 years from moving to holding a passport (ABC Color, 05/04/2026). That's the timeline worth planning around — not the marketing promises of "citizenship in a year." More on where those promises come from and why not to trust them is on the "Risks & Difficulties" page.

Methodological note There are no officially fixed standards for how long the DNM takes to process an application itself (exactly how long it takes to issue Temporal status or a Paraguayan Cédula after filing the full package) in open sources as of this writing — it's internal administrative practice that can change. The figures on this page (about 8 weeks for Temporal, 3–6 days on average for the RUC) reflect our experience, not an official standard: we track each case individually and tell clients the current timeline as it progresses.
