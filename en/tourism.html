Tourism: what to see in Paraguay

20 attractions across the country — from historic downtown Asunción and the country's only UNESCO site to the Itaipú dam and the Mennonite colonies of the Chaco. This isn't glossy tourist copy: every entry has a source and an honest caveat where the sources disagree, on the same principle as the rest of the site.

Asunción and surroundings

Palacio de los López

The official residence of the President of Paraguay on the riverbank — the most recognizable architectural landmark of downtown Asunción, especially striking under evening floodlighting.

Learn more →

Panteón Nacional de los Héroes

The mausoleum of Paraguay's national heroes in downtown Asunción, with a dome in the spirit of the Paris Les Invalides — free admission and one of the capital's most symbolic sites.

Learn more →

Catedral Metropolitana de la Asunción

The capital's active cathedral, dedicated to the Assumption of the Virgin Mary — the feast the city itself is named after.

Learn more →

Costanera de Asunción

The waterfront promenade along the capital's bay, opened in its present form in 2012 — views of the bay and the presidential palace, a spot for evening walks.

Learn more →

Barrio San Jerónimo

Asunción's most colorful neighborhood — painted façades and a famous mural staircase with a panoramic view of the city.

Learn more →

Jardín Botánico y Zoológico de Asunción

A 110-hectare park with century-old trees and a small zoo — one of the few genuinely green corners of the capital.

Learn more →

Casa de la Independencia

A house museum in the historic center where, in May 1811, the declaration of Paraguay's independence from Spain was prepared.

Learn more →

Museo del Barro

A museum of pre-Columbian and contemporary Paraguayan art — Guaraní ceramics and textiles displayed alongside the work of contemporary artists.

Learn more →

Mercado 4

Asunción's largest public market — not a tourist attraction but a place to try real Paraguayan food, including chipa.

Learn more →

Aldea Maká

A settlement of the indigenous Maká people near Asunción — a look at community life and craft goods bought directly from residents.

Learn more →

Southeast: the missions, Itaipú, the triple border

The Jesuit missions of Trinidad and Jesús de Tavarangue

Paraguay's only UNESCO World Heritage Site — ruins of 17th–18th-century Jesuit missions near the border with Argentina.

Learn more →

The Itaipú Dam

One of the world's largest hydroelectric plants, on the border with Brazil — it supplies about 80% of Paraguay's electricity, and visiting from the Paraguayan side is free.

Learn more →

Ciudad del Este and the Friendship Bridge

Paraguay's third-largest city, on the triple border with Brazil and Argentina — duty-free shopping and the Friendship Bridge connecting the two countries.

Learn more →

Saltos del Monday

A waterfall about 45 meters high near Ciudad del Este — one of the last surviving fragments of Atlantic Forest in this part of the country.

Learn more →

Costanera de Encarnación

A 27-km promenade along the Paraná River, with a beach and views of Posadas, Argentina — the hub of Encarnación's famous carnival.

Learn more →

Center and northeast

Basílica de Caacupé

Paraguay's main center of Catholic pilgrimage, 50 km from Asunción — on December 8, hundreds of thousands of pilgrims gather here.

Learn more →

Cerro Corá National Park

Paraguay's largest protected area, on the border with Brazil — site of the final battle of the Paraguayan War and ancient petroglyphs.

Learn more →

Ybycuí National Park

A park 150 km from Asunción with a waterfall and a 19th-century ironworks that now operates as a museum.

Learn more →

San Bernardino and Lake Ypacaraí

A resort town on Lake Ypacaraí, founded by German settlers in 1881 — the summer retreat of Greater Asunción, with breweries and German cuisine.

Learn more →

Chaco

Filadelfia and the Mennonite colonies

The administrative center of the Chaco region, founded by Mennonites who fled the USSR in the 1930s — a museum of the region's history and colonial architecture amid dry forest.

Learn more →
