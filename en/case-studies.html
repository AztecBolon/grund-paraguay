Case Studies: typical stories of relocating to Paraguay

We don't give client names or publish photos — that's how our confidentiality agreement, which we sign with everyone who reaches out to us, works. Instead, here are typical, generalized stories: scenarios that recur in our practice, without details that would let anyone identify a specific person.

A solo professional working remotely

An IT specialist working remotely for foreign companies filed for temporary residency (Residencia Temporal) shortly after relocating. The main difficulty wasn't the residency itself but opening a bank account: some bank requirements assume you already hold a Paraguayan Cédula (Cédula) — not just a filed residency application — so for several months payments ran through alternative payment services until the Cédula came through. After obtaining the Temporal, the obligation arose to register an RUC and file regular tax returns — something the client now handles through the ongoing support subscription (see the "Services" page), including zero returns in periods with no Paraguayan-source income.

A family with children

A couple with two school-age children gathered residency documents for the whole family at once: clean criminal record certificates, birth and marriage certificates — all apostilled, with some documents additionally requiring translation by a sworn translator registered in Paraguay, since they weren't in Spanish. The process took longer than the family expected at the outset — gathering and legalizing documents for several family members almost always takes longer than for a single applicant. After obtaining the Temporal, the family locked in mandatory monthly RUC compliance for the primary applicant.

An investor

The client chose the SUACE route with an investment in a production project (from 70,000 USD, with a business plan and the creation of formal jobs) instead of the standard Residencia Temporal route. The investor certificate (CIE) was issued within the officially stated timeframe — 5 business days from submission of the complete package; permanent residency itself required several additional months on top, which was communicated to the client up front rather than sprung on them as a surprise after payment.

A business owner

The client was considering buying an existing service business. Because the market for buying and selling businesses in Paraguay is small and not covered by major international deal trackers, there was no standard valuation data to compare against — the valuation was done individually, with due diligence on the offer and support through the handover stage, rather than against a ready-made benchmark.

An honest account of first-year difficulties

A separate category of stories isn't about deals at all, but about adjusting to life in the country (a detailed, non-sensationalized breakdown of risks is on the "Risks & Difficulties" page). One client faced a seasonal dengue spike in their first months in the country and learned firsthand why we specifically recommend health insurance that covers emergency hospitalization: private clinics in Paraguay typically require payment upfront. Another client saw their wait for the transfer to permanent residency stretch out because the document package under the new requirements had to be supplemented after clarification requests from the migration service — the process took longer than the client had expected based on the general "one year" relocation promises they carried in from elsewhere before contacting us.
